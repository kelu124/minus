# Session 08 — 2026-11-02 — known 1 MHz tone at the ADC node

Board 2, rp 0.1.27 / pic 0.1.19 (proto 5). Owner injected a **1 MHz square wave (DS203) into RF7**
(IPEX socket on the PIC32_ADC node = AD1AN9 = `acq … 9`; H6 is the same net). Netlist check first:
the node carries only C94 56 pF, D1 BAT54S clamp to GND/+3V3_AVDD, R46+R45 (200 Ω) back to the
front end, RF7 and H6. The TODO's "ADC SMA" was wrong — the SMAs (RF4/RF9) are on the pulser and
transducer side; H3 = TRANSDUCER (behind the MD0100 T/R switch + OA1, and exposed to the pulser),
H4 = OA1IN+ (RX node).

Signal as seen by the ADC: ~220 LSB p-p (0.18 V) around 1.3 V, each plateau decaying (high-pass
τ ≈ 1.4 µs somewhere in the coupling) — amplitude is small but edges are one-sample sharp, which
is all the timing test needs. Captures and `period.py` in `bench/2026-11-02-tone/`.

## Interpretation
1. **20 Msps DMA path: proven** with a real signal. Samples per period exact; the 21s are the
   FRC (+0.5 %) vs the DS203 clock.
2. **DMA ceiling = 20 Msps proven** (`acq 4` = `acq 8`).
3. **CPU loop at 25 ns: at 7 of 9 phases it reads each conversion twice** (the "40 Msps" is an
   illusion). Phases 3 and 7 (and phase 3 is not even repeatable: 1 of 2 runs) give an
   un-duplicated 40-per-period waveform, so the ADC *is* producing distinct samples every 25 ns —
   but those reads return the right AC waveform with a constant **+425 LSB offset**. Same
   amplitude, different DC: the read is catching something other than the final corrected result
   (hypothesis: the pre-offset-correction value / an intermediate write). Not trustworthy as-is.
   **Conclusion: the 160 MHz 4-cycle loop cannot be a product path.** 40 Msps raw needs the
   200 MHz CPU (5-cycle loop, PLL2 for the 320 MHz ADC clock) and then a re-test with this tone.
4. **`acqil 16` runs the DMA exactly at its ceiling** (two channels × one transfer per 100 ns =
   one transfer per 50 ns = the measured DMA cost). Whether DMA1 keeps up depends on the start
   phase between the two DMA requests: 3 of 4 runs today lost every other CH1 trigger (CH1 at
   200 ns, fill doubles). The session-06 19.87 M figure was a lucky phase. Fix options: 200 MHz
   CPU (DMA 40 ns → 20 % margin), or detect-and-retry on fill time, or div 20 (5 slots, uneven
   comb — session 06).
5. New intermittent: `acq 8` occasionally fills in 218–232 µs with scrambled periods (2 of 5).
   Candidates: an interrupt/SFR access during the capture (UART RX from the host?), or the ADC
   state left by `acqcpu`. Reproduce with repeated `acq 8 0 9` and log the fill time.

## Also discussed (before the bench)
- Why 40 Msps exists but is unused (memory 0011 recap) and whether 200 MHz CPU is an overclock:
  **no** — DS70005592 rates the part DC–200 MHz (OS25 TCY min 5 ns, 3.0–3.6 V), flash wait states
  fixed at 3. We run 160 MHz only because one PLL must give CPU Fout and ADC VCO/(2·INTDIV): 640 MHz
  → 160/320 fits, 1000 MHz → 200/250 or 200/500. Cheap step: VCO 1000, CPU 200, ADC 250 MHz (32 ns
  conversion), DMA 40 ns → **clean 25 Msps via DMA, no PLL2**. Full step: PLL2 (VCO 640 → 320 MHz,
  CLKGEN6 source 1000 "PLL2 VCO DIV") + 5-cycle loop. Side effects: every FCY-derived constant
  (SCCP tick 6.25 → 5 ns, heartbeat, NOP poll) shifts; 44–95 mA at 200 MHz.

## Part 2 — clock change to CPU 200 MHz / ADC 250 MHz (pic 0.1.20 / rp 0.1.28)

Owner: "do the clock change to 200MHz". Done the single-PLL way (no PLL2 yet), which is the
datasheet's own PTG/ADC example (`clocks_initialize`, DS70005592 §"PTG" ≈ line 62310 of the
markdown): FRC 8 MHz × FBDIV 125 = VCO 1000 MHz, POSTDIV1 5 → Fout 200 MHz (CLKGEN1 CPU, CLKGEN12
SCCP), VCO1DIV INTDIV 2 → 250 MHz (CLKGEN6 ADC; TAD = input/4 = 62.5 MHz, §18 "The input ADC module
clock is divided by four", AD50 TAD 12.5–125 ns). Code: `firmware/pic32/main.c` `clocks_init()`;
constants `firmware/pic32/acq.h` (`ACQ_TICK_NS` 100 → 80, `ACQ_TRIG_DIV` 8 → 4), SAMC auto math in
`firmware/pic32/acq.c` `samc_for_div()` (5 ns counts, 24 ns conversion, 8 + 32·n ns sampling), RP
strings/defaults in `firmware/rp2354/main.c` (`cmd_acq`, `cmd_acqcpu`, `cmd_acqil`, `help`).
UART1 needed nothing (`U1BRG = FPB/115200` follows `FCY`); `ping` 1.2 ms as before.

So the DMA cost scaled with the bus clock (50 → 40 ns, 4 standard-bus clocks) exactly as memory 0011
predicted; what did **not** survive is "div 8 = the ceiling": a trigger period equal to the DMA
cost is a razor edge (it worked at 160 MHz by luck of phase, like `acqil`). The robust recipe is
to over-trigger (div 4) and let the ADC's own 32 ns busy time decimate.

## Part 3 — "check the gain on that second op amp (it's supposed to be controlled)"

Netlist (`kicad-cli sch export netlist` + `.claude/skills/kicad-netlist-reading/nets.py`, queries
`net OA`, `pins U11 U12 U13 U14 U18 R49 J6 RF13 JP6 JP7`): **OA2** of the PIC is a non-inverting stage
on **OA1OUT** (pin 18); its inverting input (pin 17, also TMS) is the **wiper of U11 MCP4531-103E**
(10 kΩ, 7-bit, I2C 0x2E, A0 = GND) with **R49 1 kΩ to GND**; the pot's terminal A is **OA2OUT =
FE_A_OUT** (pin 16, J6.2, RF13 IPEX), terminal B open → rheostat feedback. Gain = 1 + R_AW/1k with
R_AW = 10k·(128−n)/128 + 75 Ω: ×11.07 (n = 0) … ×6.08 (n = 64) … ×1.07 (n = 128). The other
controlled gain on the board is the **AD8338 VGA U10**, driven via JP6/JP7 by U18 (MCP4531 0x2F) / U14
(MCP4131 SPI) / the MCP4728 DAC. OA2OUT is **AD2AN1 only** — ADC1 cannot read it (TODO: ADC2 capture).

Firmware (commit after e8c8c4e; files `firmware/rp2354/main.c` `cmd_i2c`/`cmd_pot`/`mcp4531_*`,
`firmware/rp2354/CMakeLists.txt` + hardware_i2c, `firmware/pic32/main.c` `AMP2` handler; pic 0.1.21 /
rp 0.1.29, proto 6):
```
rpcli "i2c"            →  0x2E MCP4531 U11, 0x2F MCP4531 U18, 0x60 MCP4728 U13  (no OLED at 0x3C)
rpcli "pot 0"          →  wiper 64 -> R_AW ~5075 ohm -> OA2 gain ~ x6.08   (power-on default, volatile)
rpcli "pot 0 0"        →  wiper 0   -> x11.07 (set, read back)
rpcli "pot 0 128"      →  wiper 128 -> x1.07
rpcli "raw AMP2 1"     →  AMP2 1   (OA2 on; off at boot)
```
MCP4531 protocol used: write = [0x00|D8][D7:0] (volatile wiper 0), read = command 0x0C then 2 bytes.

**Finding that matters:** the MCP4531 datasheet (filed as
`pdfs/datasheets/MCP453X-455X-463X-465X-I2C-digipot-datasheet-DS22096B.{pdf,md}`) gives a −3 dB
bandwidth of **1 MHz for the 10 kΩ part** (mid code, 30 pF load), terminal capacitance 75 pF, wiper
capacitance 120 pF. As the feedback element of OA2 that is a low-pass below any 3–5 MHz echo: the stage
gain collapses toward ×1 above ~1 MHz. Hardware rethink needed (memory 0012 lists options).

**Not measured yet** (needs the owner's hands): inject the tone at J6.1 with `raw AMP1 0`, `raw AMP2 1`,
sweep `pot 0`, read J6.2 / RF13 with the DS203 — or U.FL RF13 → RF7 and `acq 4 0 9` so the ADC sees
OA2OUT directly. Procedure in memory 0012.

## Part 4 — alternatives to the MCP4531 for the OA2 gain (discussion, no code)

Q: does J6.1 have an IPEX? **No** — the OA1OUT net is J5.3, J6.1, R48 and PIC pins 8/18 only. IPEX sockets
near the chain: RF5 (OA1IN+ / RX node), RF13 (FE_A_OUT = OA2OUT), RF7 (PIC32_ADC node). So the OA2 test input
goes in on J6.1 or J5.3 (with `raw AMP1 0`), output out of RF13 (scope, or U.FL to RF7 + `acq 4 0 9`).

Q: alternatives to the MCP4531? First pass (web check of digipot/PGA bandwidths): AD5273 1 kΩ ≈ 6 MHz,
AD8400 1 kΩ ≈ 5 MHz (code-dependent; 10 kΩ parts 0.6–1 MHz), MCP6S91 PGA 1–18 MHz, MCP6S21 12 MHz,
MAX9939 2–3 MHz, or move the variable gain to the AD8338 VGA already on the devkit.

**Owner constraint: BOM cost and simple design; the core is RP2354 + PIC32AK, the AD8338 is a non-negligible
BOM line.** Ranking under that constraint:
- **A (recommended, zero parts): GPIO-switched ground legs** on OA2 — fixed Rf (10 k), 2–3 Rg legs (10 k /
  2.2 k / 1 k) each returned to a PIC GPIO: output-low = leg on, input = leg off → ×2 / ×5.5 / ×11 and
  combinations; a few pF + tens of ohms at a < 1 k node → tens of MHz. The PIC can step the pins on a timer
  during the A-line = **stepped TGC for free** (sub-µs glitch per step).
- B (cents): same topology with a 74HC4053 / SGM3157 switch in the leg if GPIO leakage/glitch is a problem.
- C (fiddly): JFET voltage-controlled resistor in the leg driven by the PIC DAC = continuous TGC, nonlinear.
- D: no analog gain control — fixed ×11 into the 12-bit (72 dB) ADC at 25 Msps and TGC in software; A's
  single GPIO step covers near-field saturation.
Decision direction: **A with D behind it**; AD8338, both MCP4531, both MCP4131 and the MCP4728 leave the
minimal BOM. OA1 × OA2 still gives up to ×121.

**Devkit test of A (rework, owner):** lift U11; 10 k between J6.2 and J6.3 (feedback); 1 k and 2.2 k from J6.3
to spare PIC GPIOs on H2, e.g. H2.1 (RD6) and H2.4 (RD5). Then Claude adds a `GAIN <mask>` link command (TRIS/LAT
on those pins) and the steps are measured with the 1 MHz tone (J6.1 in, RF13 → RF7 out, `acq 4 0 9`).
